The approach

Forecast the move, not the aftermath

PriceLeap models every tracked competitor separately: how often they move, how deep they cut, which days they favour and what usually precedes a change.

Each forecast is published with a probability, a price range and a window — so a 78% call and a 40% call are treated differently rather than both becoming an alert.

High-confidence forecasts pre-stage a response in the Pricing Decision Engine. It publishes the moment the predicted move is confirmed, and expires quietly if it never arrives.

How it works

How PriceLeap forecasts a competitor's next move

01STEP 01

Learn each competitor's pattern

Years of observed price changes are decomposed per competitor into cadence, typical depth, preferred weekday and the events that tend to precede a move.

Patterns are held per competitor and per category. A marketplace seller repricing hourly and a national retailer repricing monthly are never modelled the same way.

02STEP 02

Forecast the next move

For each SKU and competitor the model returns a probability, an expected price, an interval and a window — the four things needed to decide whether to act early.

Accuracy degrades with distance, and the forecast says so. Past fourteen days most categories are reported for planning rather than used to stage prices.

03STEP 03

Pre-stage the response, then score the call

Forecasts above your threshold stage a price behind your existing rules and floors. It publishes on confirmation of the predicted move, or expires without touching anything.

Every forecast is scored against what actually happened. Hit rate, timing error and depth error are reported per competitor and per horizon, and feed the next model run.
